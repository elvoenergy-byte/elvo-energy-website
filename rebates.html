What's actually available right now.

State and federal solar incentives change fairly often, and eligibility depends on your household income, property value and postcode. Here's the current picture — we'll confirm exactly what applies to you when you enquire.

Solar Homes Program (Victoria)

The state government's main residential solar incentive, run by Solar Victoria.

Solar panel (PV) rebate

A point-of-sale rebate on solar panel installation for eligible owner-occupiers, available for existing homes, homes under construction and rental properties.

Up to $1,400

Matching interest-free loan

An optional interest-free loan equal to your rebate amount, to further reduce the upfront cost.

Up to $1,400

Hot water rebate

Covers eligible hot water systems, including heat pump hot water — the amount depends on whether the product is locally made.

Up to $1,400

Income cap: From 1 July 2026, combined household income must be $150,000 or less per year to qualify for the solar panel and hot water rebates. You'll also need a property valued under $3 million, no prior solar rebate on the home, and installation by a Clean Energy Council–approved installer using approved products.

Battery incentives

Victoria's old interest-free battery loan closed in May 2025. Battery support now runs through a federal program instead.

Cheaper Home Batteries Program (federal)

An upfront discount applied directly to your invoice, worth roughly 30% of your battery system's cost — calculated per usable kilowatt-hour of storage rather than as a flat amount.

~$370 / kWh

Federal Small-scale Technology Certificates (STCs)

Separate from the state programs, most solar and battery installations also generate STCs under the federal Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme — effectively a discount your installer applies at the point of sale. The number of certificates a system earns reduces each year as the scheme winds down toward 2030, so the value on offer is highest now and gets smaller over time.

Stacked together, a solar and battery system installed in Victoria can currently draw on all three of these at once — the state panel rebate, the federal battery discount, and STCs — which is where most of the real savings come from.
